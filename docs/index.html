Go-to-market plan · UAE launch

Kestrelle lands in Dubai. Here are the first 8 weeks.

Kestrelle is a made-up skincare brand with three products: a light SPF 50 fluid, a barrier serum and a gentle cleanser. It sells online in the US and UK and wants the UAE next, starting with Dubai. This is the plan I'd hand the team: who we sell to, where we show up, what goes out each week, how creators fit in, where the money goes and how we'll know it's working.

01 · The short version

Six decisions, up front.

If someone only reads one section, it should be this one. Everything below explains how we got here and how we'd check it.

  1. 1

    Lead with the SPF fluid

    It's the easiest product to explain in a sunny city, and it's the one people use up fastest, so it's the best road to a second order. The serum and cleanser ride along in a launch set.

  2. 2

    Paperwork before posts

    In Dubai, cosmetics have to be registered in Dubai Municipality's Montaji system before they're advertised or sold, and ads and promotions need pre-approval too.3 Nothing goes live until that's done.

  3. 3

    Creators first, ads second

    Creators carry weeks 3 to 6. Paid social then puts money behind the posts that already work. Every creator we pay needs a UAE Media Council advertiser permit.4

  4. 4

    English and Arabic from day one

    The site, the ads and the creator briefs go out in both. Arabic copy is written or checked by a native speaker, not just translated.

  5. 5

    Launch a week before Ramadan

    Launch on Monday, February 1, 2027. Ramadan is expected to start around February 8,6 so evening routines and Eid gifting become a second wave, not the first impression.

  6. 6

    Judge it on orders, not views

    At week 8 the call comes down to cost per first order and how many people order again. Views and followers are useful signals, not the goal.

What that means for the plan

  • The real pool is smaller than the headline. Women are roughly a third of the population and of social accounts, so reach and budget estimates start from that third, not the full totals.
  • Reach is not users. DataReportal itself flags that some platform figures top 100% of adults. I use these numbers to rank channels, never to forecast sales.
  • TikTok and Instagram are the core. Snapchat gets a smaller test budget so we can see if it pulls its weight.
  • A big visitor flow. Travel sizes and hotel or duty-free retail are out of scope for launch, but worth a look once the core works.

Assumptions to check, not facts

  • People here want an SPF that feels light in heat and humidity and sits well under makeup. Sampling and creator feedback in weeks 2 to 4 test this.
  • AED 119 for the SPF is in line with other mid-priced imported sunscreens. Check real shelf prices before launch.
  • Shoppers expect fast delivery and options like cash on delivery or pay-later at checkout. Confirm with the fulfillment and payments partners.

03 · Positioning and audience

Who it's for, and the one thing to say.

The three groups are working sketches built from the brand's product, not research findings. The first month of sales and comments will sharpen them.

Positioning

For people in the UAE who wear sunscreen every day, Kestrelle Daily Veil is the SPF 50 that feels like nothing in the heat. It's a thin fluid that goes on clear and sits well under makeup, so you don't skip it on busy mornings.

Every product claim in ads and briefs gets checked against the brand's own test data before it's used.

A

The office regular

25 to 40 · works in an office · wears makeup most days

Wants: one quick step in the morning that won't pill under foundation.

Find them on: Instagram Reels, Google search

“One step. Goes on clear. Fine under makeup.”

B

The new-launch fan

20 to 30 · follows beauty creators in Arabic and English

Wants: to try what's new, ideally with a creator code.

Find them on: TikTok, Snapchat, creator links

“The sunscreen that doesn't feel like sunscreen.”

C

The outdoors crowd

25 to 45 · runs, plays padel, spends weekends outside

Wants: something comfortable to wear and easy to reapply.

Find them at: run clubs, gyms, padel courts (sampling)

“Light enough to put back on at half time.”

Four things we keep saying

  1. Feels like nothing. Show the texture up close, every time.
  2. Every single day. Built for a habit, not a beach trip.
  3. Plays well with makeup. Prove it in real routines, not studio shots.
  4. Three products, one routine. Keep it simple to buy.

Words we don't use

  • miracle
  • whitening
  • fairness
  • clinically proven*
  • cures
  • best in the UAE

*Only if there's a study behind it that we can show. No medical claims, and no promises about results on anyone's skin.

07 · Creator outreach

Lots of small creators, a few bigger ones.

Smaller creators are cheaper, easier to brief and more likely to actually use the product. Fees below are example figures to agree with the brand.

How it works

  1. Find. Search beauty and routine hashtags in English and Arabic. Read the comments to make sure they're real people.
  2. Check. Media Council permit number on the profile, mostly UAE audience (ask for an analytics screenshot), no sudden follower jumps.45
  3. Reach out. A short, specific message like the one here. Product first, no pressure.
  4. Brief. One page: product facts, the claims we can and can't make, and how to label the post as an ad.
  5. Approve. We check drafts against the approved claims before anything goes up.
  6. Pay and track. A code and link for each creator, paid within 14 days of posting.

On every brief

  • Show the texture on skin, up close
  • Use it in your real routine, makeup on top
  • Label it as an ad, permit number on your profile
  • No before and after photos
  • No claims beyond the approved list
  • No “best” or “number one” comparisons

Test 1 · Weeks 5 and 6

Which hook sells it?

Same creator footage, three different first lines: “Feels like nothing,” “Goes on clear,” “Under makeup all day.” Equal spend, AED 1,500 each, over 5 days.

Call it: lowest cost per add to cart once each version has at least 60. If two are within 10%, keep both and test again.

Test 2 · Weeks 5 and 6

Set or single?

The 3-product launch set at AED 299 against the SPF alone at AED 119 with a free mini cleanser. Same audience, same creative.

Call it: the one with more profit per order after ad cost, not just the higher order value.

Test 3 · Weeks 5 to 7

Arabic first or English first?

To an Arabic-language audience, Arabic-first video and captions against English-first with Arabic subtitles.

Call it: cost per first order. If Arabic-first wins clearly, it becomes the default for that audience.

10 · Risks

What could go wrong, and the plan B.

None of these are reasons not to launch. They're the things worth checking every week.

Registration takes longer

Montaji approval sets the start date. Start it first, and don't book creators or pay for ads with fixed dates until it's through.

A creator isn't permitted

Only creators with a valid advertiser permit get paid deals. Check the number, keep a screenshot, check again before posting.

Heat in transit

Confirm storage and delivery conditions with the fulfillment partner, and spot-check a few orders in week 5.

Tone during Ramadan

Calm, routine-led content. No loud countdowns or stunts. Gifting talk saves for the run-up to Eid.

The audience is smaller than it looks

Cap how often the same person sees an ad, and refresh creative every 10 days so we don't wear people out.

The tests don't call

If there's not enough data by week 6, keep the reserve and run the test one more week rather than guessing.
